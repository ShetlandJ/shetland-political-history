# The first Zetland County Council, 1890–1892: meeting notes

Swept 2026-10-01 from the British Newspaper Archive (Shetland Times, every Saturday issue from
8 Feb 1890 to 31 Dec 1892; none missing). One entry per reported meeting of the Council or its
committees, with the issue, page and article numbers; items marked `[hurdle]` are the
difficulties the Council ran into. These notes are the source of the artifact
**The First Zetland County Council, 1890–1892** (https://claude.ai/artifact/KWcQF6jA2KUbKSKASYLCme).

Citations are written `ST 15 Feb 1890 p3 a070–076`; the viewer link is
`https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0000666%2F18900215&page=0003&article=070`.
Meeting days are inferred from the report's wording unless stated. Nothing here has gone into the
ledger, the DB or a correction script.



## 1890

### county Thu 13 Feb 1890 — ST 15 Feb 1890 p3 a070–076
type: first
headline: The first meeting of the County Council
note: "held on Thursday, the 13th February current, in the County Hall at noon" (ST 15 Feb 1890 p3 a069)
- Present: John Bruce (Convener of the County), A. S. Stove, J. J. Meiklejohn, J. McCullie, R. Henderson, T. Anderson, J. Robertson, T. Gifford, S. T. Duncan, Alex. Sandison, Z. M. Hamilton, Ar. Laurenson, John Robertson jun., John Anderson, A. C. Hay, J. Anderson, Revs G. Clark, J. Love and D. Gray; J. C. Grierson ex officio. Grierson unanimously elected chairman.
- [hurdle] PROTESTS AGAINST TWO RETURNS: the Deputy Returning Officer reported protests: George H. Bruce against S. T. Duncan's return for Sandwick as disqualified, and A. J. Garriock against the declaration of the poll for the southern division of Sandsting and Aithsting. Both sent to the Secretary for Scotland.
- [hurdle] LIEUT. BRUCE AT THE TABLE: Lieut. Bruce appeared and protested that Duncan was "an undischarged bankrupt" and that he himself was the only qualified councillor for Sandwick. Long wrangle whether he had any standing; Robertson: no locus standi. Minute written without naming him; he left the meeting.
- ACCEPTANCE OF OFFICE: letters accepting office from W. A. Bruce (Whalsay and Skerries), A. J. Hay (Tingwall), J. H. Sutherland (Unst North), and a telegram from H. H. Bell (Nesting and Lunnasting).
- [hurdle] RESIGNATION OF A COUNCILLOR: Charles Hoseason (Delting North) resigns because of his Post Office connection; the defeated William Pole's proposer and seconder claim Pole should now be declared elected. Letter allowed to lie; vacancies reported to the Secretary for Scotland.
- [hurdle] THE CONVENER OF THE COUNTY: John Bruce was Convener of the County (ex officio member) when he stood and was elected for Dunrossness South. Laurenson cites section 109: the four ex officio members are "in addition to" the elected ones, so Bruce's election left the division unrepresented; "Heads win, tails you lose". Referred to the Secretary for Scotland, unanimously.
- PROVISIONAL FINANCE COMMITTEE: Grierson, J. B. Anderson, John Robertson jun., Laurenson, Stove, A. C. Hay, Meiklejohn and John Bruce; the Clerk to open an account with the Commercial Bank and overdraw it up to £100.
- THE ASSESSMENTS: to be levied by the old bodies and collected by the County Council.
- THE AVERAGE TAX: Sheriff Thoms's letter on striking the average rate left with the landowners and the Sheriffs.

### county Thu 13 Mar 1890 — ST 15 Mar 1890 p2 a073–076
type: statutory
headline: Meeting of the Provisional Council
note: "on Thursday at noon"; nominations for the Walls and Fetlar vacancies closed "Friday, 14th inst."
- Present: J. C. Grierson (presiding), A. J. Hay, J. J. R. Meiklejohn, J. Robertson, J. H. Sutherland, S. T. Duncan, A. S. Stove, Ar. Laurenson, J. Robertson jun., J. B. Anderson, A. C. Hay, John Bruce, Rev. D. Gray.
- CHAIRMAN OF THE NEW COUNCIL: John Bruce, former Convener of the County, "connected with county business for upwards of 30 years", appointed. J. C. Grierson Vice-Chairman, "a young man of promise".
- APPOINTMENT OF COMMITTEES: the town members had met beforehand, guided by a report by Nicolson of Glenbervie (two committees enough). Finance Committee enlarged by five (W. A. Bruce, R. H. Bell, Z. M. Hamilton, A. J. Hay, Rev. D. Gray) to report on the duties and salaries of the existing officers and the money needed to take over; the whole Provisional Council formed into an Administrative Committee (convener Bruce) on the business transferred, dividing the county into districts (with representations to the Boundary Commissioners), standing orders, and arrangements before the appointed day, 15 May.
- [hurdle] THE VACANCIES: the Marquess of Lothian ordered new elections for Walls South, Walls North and Fetlar (no nominations in February).
- [hurdle] MR HOSEASON'S RESIGNATION: Whitehall (26 Feb): the Delting North vacancy is for the County Council to fill, but the Provisional Council isn't the County Council until 15 May, so the seat "must continue" vacant till then.
- THE SANDWICH ELECTION: Lothian returns the protest against S. T. Duncan; not a case he is called on to adjudge.
- MR BRUCE AND THE CONVENERSHIP: the Secretary for Scotland holds Bruce's election valid. The Clerk wrote again pointing out Bruce was actually Convener, not "late convener", at the election; no reply yet. Bruce: "I don't claim two votes, you know."

### county Thu 1 May 1890 — ST 3 May 1890 p2 a054
type: special
headline: Adjourned meeting of the Provisional Council: two claimants for Sandsting
note: Printed at the end of the Road Trustees' report (p2 a054), with no day; Thu 1 May inferred (the Commissioners of Supply met "on Wednesday" the same week). Found 2026-10-01 while checking the chapter; the first sweep missed it.
- Present: Convener John Bruce (presiding), A. C. Hay, J. B. Anderson, John Robertson jun., Arthur Laurenson, A. S. Stove, Z. M. Hamilton, John Robertson, J. McCullie, A. J. Garriock, P. Georgeson, J. C. Grierson, J. J. R. Meiklejohn, Revs Love, Clark and Gray.
- THE NEW ELECTIONS: the Secretary for Scotland's order for new elections in the two Sandsting and Aithsting divisions read, with the Deputy Returning Officer's word that James Anderson, Aithsness, was elected for the northern division and Archibald J. Garriock for the southern.
- [hurdle] McCULLIE'S PROTESTS: McCullie, elected for the southern division in February, asks whether the Secretary of State means to give Aithsting (southern) two councillors. His letter of 20 Mar protests against "any new election" because he is "the legally-elected Councillor"; a second protest (14 Apr) objects to any declaration of Garriock's election, the roll not being "properly made up". The Secretary's office had only referred him to section 108; his supporters had sent a memorial.
- [hurdle] WHO SITS FOR SANDSTING: Anderson moves that McCullie isn't entitled to sit and his name be struck from the sederunt; Grierson moves instead that Garriock be excluded. Four votes each; the Convener's casting vote carries Anderson's motion. Clark's motion to lay "all the peculiar circumstances" before the Secretary for Scotland agreed. McCullie withdrew.

### county Thu 22 May 1890 — ST 24 May 1890 p3 a041–048
type: appointed
headline: The first meeting of the County Council of Zetland (after the appointed day, 15 May)
note: Day lost in the OCR ("held in the County Hall on [...] noon"); Thu 22 May from the wiki text (fix_parse_errors #11). Afternoon and evening sederunts.
- Present: Convener John Bruce, Vice-Convener J. C. Grierson + 20 (A. Laurenson, J. J. R. Meiklejohn, A. J. Garriock, Z. M. Hamilton, T. Gifford, J. H. Sutherland, J. Robertson, T. Anderson, R. Henderson, S. T. Duncan, A. S. Stove, W. A. Bruce, R. H. Bell, John Robertson jun., J. B. Anderson, A. C. Hay, A. J. Hay, Revs Geo. Clark, John Love, Jas. A. Campbell).
- APPOINTMENT OF CONVENER: Bruce takes the chair as Convener, thanked for his work in the Provisional Council, "whose labours had now happily ended".
- [hurdle] OBJECTIONS TO COUNCILLORS: Grierson protests formally against A. J. Garriock's name on the sederunt and asks whether S. T. Duncan is qualified. Duncan hands in a petition for recall of his sequestration. Anderson: the sequestration ran till 19 Mar, after the election, so he was disqualified. Rev. Clark warns against "long legal disputes at the start". Duncan allowed to keep his seat, 11 to 7.
- [hurdle] FILLING UP OF VACANCIES: James Inkster, Brae, appointed for Delting North. Walls North (Sandness): rival proposals (Lieut. G. H. Bruce; D. Inglis; R. T. C. Scott of Melby); a ratepayers' requisition for J. M. Goudie, Lerwick merchant; Hay objects that Goudie lives 40 miles away; Anderson doubts it is a casual vacancy they can fill at all. Superseded; the Secretary for Scotland to be told the Council proposes to fill it.
- APPOINTMENT OF OFFICIALS: Archibald Sutherland County Clerk (£65) and County Road Clerk (£20); R. B. Hunter County Collector (2% commission); D. M. Ross surveyor to the County Road Board (£100). Advertise for a veterinary surgeon, perhaps combined with sanitary inspector; Levie reappointed veterinary inspector for two months.
- [hurdle] APPOINTMENT OF ASSESSOR: Mr Young, paid £100 last year, now wants £200. Grierson moves a cut; Anderson warns that if they offer less he will refuse and they'll have to ask the Treasury for another assessor at greater expense. £100 kept, 11 to 9. John Brown inspector of weights and measures (£12). A medical officer to be advertised for, to visit each district twice a year.
- APPOINTMENT OF COMMITTEES: Standing Joint Committee (Alex. Sandison, Thos. Anderson, Geo. Clark, J. Robertson jun., A. Laurenson, Z. M. Hamilton, A. J. Garriock); Valuation, Lunacy Board, Prison and other committees.
- EVENING SEDERUNT: bank accounts opened: General Purposes £73, Police £150, Road Management £600, Public Health £100 (£1,580 in all, as printed). County Clerk paid twenty guineas for bringing the Act into operation. Parliamentary Bills and General Purposes Committees.
- [hurdle] DIVISION OF THE COUNTY INTO DISTRICTS: five districts proposed for roads and public health; the Convener would rather have one. Agreed to make the whole county one district, less the Burgh of Lerwick, rated by parish: "only an experiment", to be revisited if it doesn't work.
- STANDING ORDERS: a long list adopted.
- THE SEAL OF THE COUNCIL: designed through Laurenson with Dr Joseph Anderson, York Powell and J. T. Irvine: a Viking dragon ship, "Hjaltlands Althing" round a rock (the "Old Rock"), and the Norse motto "Með lögum skal land byggja" from Njal's saga. Four illuminated copies for the helpers; a flag with the seal to fly over the County Buildings when the Council sits.

### committee Mon 2 Jun 1890 — ST 7 Jun 1890 p3 a049–052
type: committee
headline: County Road Board, Valuation Committee, Standing Joint Committee and District Committee
note: "on Monday" (2 Jun); the District Committee at three o'clock the same afternoon
- Present (Road Board): Convener John Bruce + 17 (Meiklejohn, Garriock, Alex. Sandison, Gifford, J. Anderson, S. T. Duncan, T. Anderson, J. Inkster, A. J. Hay, Grierson, Stove, R. H. Bell, W. A. Bruce, J. B. Anderson, J. Robertson jun., Laurenson, Rev. Geo. Clark).
- COUNTY ROAD BOARD: A. J. Hay chairman (in G. H. B. Hay's absence). Archibald Sutherland County Road and District Clerk (£40 for the conjoined offices); R. B. Hunter, clerk of the Dunrossness District Committee, offers to resign on the appointed day so there is no "question of compensation". D. M. Ross surveyor (£100).
- THE ROADS OF THE COUNTY: Ross's return: 255 miles of district roads in all, parish by parish (Dunrossness, Sandwick, Cunningsburgh and Fair Isle 37 miles; Tingwall, Whiteness and Weisdale 37; Unst 14; North Yell and Fetlar under 10). To be printed for the next meeting.
- [hurdle] GATES ON THE ROADS: the Zetland Roads Act set 15 feet from the centre; Lord Young had held "the Zetland roads belonged to the proprietors". Bruce: the gates are "all illegal" and the Surveyor may remove them; fencing and ploughing up to the road to be stopped.
- [hurdle] UNST AND THE ONE DISTRICT: Alex. Sandison asks whether Unst can lawfully be lumped into one county district; Unst kept its own roads and the general assessment it paid was "unjust" (Grierson: Yell and Fetlar the same). Grierson: settled by the County Council, can't be gone back on; Sandison to move at the next meeting.
- [hurdle] SULLOM ROAD PETITION (34 inhabitants of Northmavine): children can't get to school in winter without a road. Robertson: no new road can be ordered except at the Road Board's annual meeting in October.
- VALUATION COMMITTEE: J. B. Anderson chairman.
- STANDING JOINT COMMITTEE (with the Commissioners of Supply): Sheriff Mackenzie chairman; it takes the place of the old Police Committee (as in Orkney). Superintendent Gray of the County Police wants an allowance for a pony: told to note what he can't recover from the Exchequer. A sum voted for lavatory accommodation for the Council.
- DISTRICT COMMITTEE: Bruce chairman; Parochial Board representatives returned (Unst John Craigie, Sandsting Gilbert Williamson, Walls R. T. C. Scott, Bressay Rev. D. Johnstone, Mid and South Yell James Mouat, Nesting Rev. W. Levie, Lerwick J. M. Goudie, Northmavine Rev. P. H. Russell, Dunrossness Rev. C. N. Baikie; Delting William Pole elected 2 Jun, ST p2 a39). Sutherland clerk.

### committee Tue 17 Jun 1890 — ST 21 Jun 1890 p3 a054–055
type: committee
headline: Lord Lothian's visit: joint committee of County and Town Councils
note: "on Tuesday at noon"
- Present: from the County Council A. J. Garriock (presiding), A. C. Hay, W. Bruce, A. S. Stove, T. Gifford, Rev. J. Love, J. C. Grierson, Rev. D. Gray, Rev. Geo. Clark, J. J. R. Meiklejohn, Ar. Laurenson, John Bruce; from the Town Council John Robertson, A. L. Laing, John Leisk, J. M. Goudie, G. G. Kay, Charles Robertson; Sheriff Mackenzie; Town Clerk Sandison.
- The Secretary for Scotland (Lothian) was to visit Shetland that week; at its last meeting the County Council had made itself a committee of the whole to work with the Town Council's committee.
- A DAILY STEAMER: Lothian to be urged to get a subsidy for "a daily direct steam service between Aberdeen and Shetland".
- [hurdle] LERWICK PIER DEBT: the harbour revenue hasn't met its costs for two years; ask the Public Works Loan Commissioners to cut the 4% interest to the Consols rate (£250 a year saved).
- GRANT IN AID OF LOCAL TAXATION: a "great relief to the poor rates"; ask that it continue.
- LIGHTS: a grant for lighthouses on the west coast, and leading lights at the boat harbours.
- A LIGHT RAILWAY from Lerwick to Scalloway.
- [hurdle] ROADS: Shetland's share of what the Government spent on Highland roads and bridges, since Shetland was left out.
- Inter-island steamers; the telegraph to the fishing districts "without any guarantee" from private people; harbours and piers where "the fishing and trade ... are not sufficient to pay" for them.

### committee Tue 24 Jun 1890 — ST 28 Jun 1890 p2 a064–070
type: special
headline: The joint committee meets Lord Lothian, Secretary for Scotland, in the County Hall
note: Lothian arrived on the Admiralty yacht Enchantress on Monday evening (23 Jun), via Fair Isle; met the committee "Tuesday noon"
- Present: the joint County and Town Council committee; Lothian with Sir George Baden-Powell MP, W. C. Dunbar (Scotch Office) and others. Met at Victoria Pier by Sheriff Mackenzie, Bruce and Garriock.
- Lothian: struck by the contrast with the Western Isles, "in favour of Shetland". Shetland had not at first been in the scope of the Highlands and Islands Commission; he has now had it included, though the Commissioners may not get there this year.
- INCREASED COMMUNICATION WITH THE SOUTH (Laurenson's memorandum): Aberdeen is 14 hours direct but the steamers take 24 or more calling at other ports; two or three a week. [hurdle] The fresh fish trade is "risky and unremunerative"; "a very large number" of fishermen have left and boats stand ashore for want of men. A Treasury grant for a daily direct steamer asked.
- [hurdle] LERWICK HARBOUR DEBT: the Harbour Trust's 1877 loan; promoters had paid the Bill's costs themselves against opposition; revenue short; lower interest asked.
- Grant in aid of local taxation; inter-insular steamers; telegraphs; harbours and piers.
- LOTHIAN'S REPLY: "thoroughly sympathising"; the telegraph to the fishing districts "should not be delayed", on the Norwegian principle; strongly for lights at the boat harbours; the rest for the Commission. [hurdle] The steamer depends on whatever grant the Commission can give. Demands "very reasonable".
- A gale kept the yacht in harbour on Wednesday and the North Isles visit was abandoned; Lothian left for Oban at 4am Thursday.

### county Thu 3 Jul 1890 — ST 5 Jul 1890 p3 a047–051; Road Board and District Committee ST 12 Jul 1890 p3 a069
type: ordinary
headline: Adjourned meeting; then the Road Board (afternoon) and District Committee (evening)

- LORD LOTHIAN'S VISIT: "very satisfactory". [hurdle] The £90,000 grant for relief of local taxation in the Highlands and Islands has been "diverted into another channel"; with the Government dropping clauses of the Local Taxation Bill, a memorial asks Lothian to restore it.

- [hurdle] VETERINARY AND SANITARY INSPECTOR: told to proceed "upon as little cost as possible" and to know the liabilities in each district first. Applications from £50 to £240; a committee to report within a month; Levie kept on as interim inspector for two more months.
- MEDICAL OFFICER FOR THE COUNTY: nine applicants, asking £110 to £600 (Dr Skae and Dr Yule of Lerwick £200 each; Dr McLennan, Scalloway, £140); to the same committee.
- SANITARY INSPECTOR: eight applicants, £60 to £130.
- ROAD BOARD (Thursday afternoon, A. J. Hay presiding): Uyeasound telegraph poles: Alex. Sandison says the road is private, built at his own expense, so the Board has no jurisdiction. The Surveyor's list of roads approved, with short-cuts kept as footpaths. [hurdle] Cattle grazing the road verges at Hayfield: the Fiscal won't prosecute without the Board's instructions; police told to protect the roads under the Roads and Bridges Act.
- DISTRICT COMMITTEE (Thursday evening, Bruce presiding): new representatives (Tingwall Rev. A. Bayne, Delting W. Pole, Fetlar and North Yell Rev. J. Bicket). [hurdle] Sandison asks £100 for the Unst roads since Unst has paid assessments "and got nothing by way of return"; Bruce: Unst's mails go over the mainland roads; "could not be entertained". Surveyor to estimate finishing two Northmavine roads (Lighthouse Croft to Eshaness; on to North Roe). To meet the first Thursday of every month.

### county Thu 21 Aug 1890 — ST 23 Aug 1890 p3 a079–085
type: ordinary
headline: Doctors, inspectors and the Boundary Commission; Road Board afterwards
note: "on Thursday at noon" (21 Aug, the regatta day; ST 9 Aug p2 a72); the Road Board "on Thursday" too
- Present: Convener John Bruce + 17 (J. B. Anderson, J. Robertson jun., A. Sandison, T. Gifford, S. T. Duncan, J. C. Grierson, T. Anderson, R. H. Bell, W. A. Bruce, J. Inkster, J. Anderson, J. Robertson, A. S. Stove, Z. M. Hamilton, J. J. R. Meiklejohn, A. J. Garriock, Rev. George Clark).
- [hurdle] THE LAVATORY ACCOMMODATION: the lowest offer (£69 10s) withdrawn; the Joint Committee asked for £100 instead of the £70 voted.
- [hurdle] APPOINTMENT OF MEDICAL OFFICERS: with an eye to Mr Peterkin of the Board of Supervision, the committee recommends none of the applicants for County Medical Officer: advertise again (at £40 a year). Eight district medical officers instead, £10–£15 each (Drs Young, Milne, Taylor, Burgess, Smith, McLennan, Skae, Stewart).
- [hurdle] WHO APPOINTS THE DOCTORS: Rev. Clark and Meiklejohn say the district appointments belong to the District Committees (with Parochial Board members); Sandison fears a precedent "taking away the powers" of the districts. - [hurdle] SANITARY AND VETERINARY INSPECTOR COMBINED: Duncan McArthur, Edinburgh, £150 inclusive, from 1 Oct, subject to the Board of Supervision. Rev. Clark wants a full-time sanitary inspector: the Public Health Act had since 1872 been "very loosely carried out", in many parts not at all. Bruce: "simply feeling their way under the new Act"; separate officers would cost much more. Clark's amendment unseconded.
- THE BOUNDARY COMMISSION: the Council forms itself into a committee with parish and school board representatives to draw up a scheme before the Commission's visit on 1 Sep.
- OCEAN PENNY POSTAGE: Henniker Heaton presented the Council's petition, "received with cheers".

- [hurdle] LOCAL TAXATION GRANT REFUSED: Lothian: any such grant would be temporary, and the sum freed by dropping the licensing clauses should probably go to the whole of Scotland.
- [hurdle] POLICE SUPERANNUATION: Orkney and Shetland police can't join the Police Superannuation (Scotland) Bill scheme, not being under Government inspection or sharing the Exchequer contribution.
- Compensation for the old sanitary inspectors: left until they claim. Notice of motion (Rev. Clark, Garriock): petition the Secretary for Scotland on skatt "and other grievances of Orkney and Shetland".
- COUNTY ROAD BOARD: the Hoswick road (Duncan) recommended for adoption at October's general meeting. [hurdle] Surveyor Ross: all 71 gates on the mainland main roads to go, "very dangerous to travellers"; Walls–Watsness, an old "meal road", needs rebuilding from end to end; estimate £1,125 for the year. Gates to a committee of the whole Board, which may take counsel's opinion. [hurdle] Mrs Simpson (Cheshire): road tax on Gloup is "gross injustice" with no road for two-thirds of the way and children wet-footed; letter left to lie.

### committee Mon 1 Sep 1890 — ST 6 Sep 1890 p2 a070 (end of article, "Boundary Commission in Lerwick")
type: special
headline: The Boundary Commission sits in the County Hall
note: "on Monday at noon"
- Present: Col. Malcolm, R.E. (chairman), Mr Pelham, Mr Hay (secretary); for the County Council J. B. Anderson, A. J. Garriock, J. J. R. Meiklejohn, with A. J. Hay, J. K. Galloway, R. D. Ganson, W. S. Smith, W. Jamieson, A. A. Porteous and others.
- [hurdle] BRESSAY, BURRA AND QUARFF: the Council wanted the parish split, but Malcolm doubts the Commission can make new parishes. Its view: leave Bressay alone, join Quarff to Lerwick and Burra to Tingwall. Arthur Hay objects that Burra members would find Tingwall meetings harder to reach than Lerwick.
- FETLAR AND NORTH YELL: petitions to separate them: a "dangerous sound" to cross, and the school board often can't get a quorum. Malcolm suggests joining North Yell to Mid and South Yell; Sandison: the road is hilly and dangerous, Mid Yell's school rate is rising, and North Yell would rather stay with Fetlar.
- Whalsay and Skerries want to be a parish apart from Nesting and Lunnasting (laid on the table).
- [hurdle] SANDSTING AND AITHSTING: the February divisions followed the county road, not the old parish boundary; "very difficult to work" and led to petitions to the Secretary for Scotland. Revert to the old boundary and the names Sandsting and Aithsting.
- Dunrossness asks for five divisions; it would mean robbing another parish: Garriock: Walls North, "because they ... declined". Withdrawn.
- [hurdle] LERWICK'S WARDS: about 100 electors in the south, 300 in the centre and 400 in the north; the Town Council to suggest fairer divisions.
- Delting asks for the old North/South boundary back, fairer by rental and voters.

### committee Mon 15 Sep 1890 — ST 20 Sep 1890 p3 a099–107 (day from the notice, ST 13 Sep 1890 p1 a005)
type: committee
headline: The County Valuation Committee's appeal court
note: Date from the advertised court, "MONDAY, the 15th day of September ... eleven o'clock"; the report gives no day
- APPEALS: several dismissed for non-appearance. A Twageos house the tenant had to leave ("the feet of the beds had gone through the bed-room floors"): cut from £23 to £12 10s. King Harald Street houses cut to £20. C. McLaughlin, Northness: only leases of 21 years or more pay proprietor's rates; the Court "not seeming at all clear", adjourned to the evening, then sustained the appeal at £50.
- [hurdle] FISHING STATIONS: Lady Nicolson's Gremista stations, valued at £200 on 48,000 crans; landings fell from 25,000 crans (1885) to 5,000 (1888) and none in 1889; only four stations used. Cut to £100.

### Retrospective (1892)
- ST Sat 26 Nov 1892, p2 a034 (leader): interest in the election "of the most languid description". In Lerwick that is natural, since the Town Council runs the burgh except its roads; in the country it "is probably the result of two-and-a-half years' experience of the present Council". To the country electors there is "but little difference" between "the new-fangled Council" and the unelected Commissioners of Supply. Business done "with great discretion" and economy, and rates lowered, "largely aided by ... Government grants". Big road schemes were drawn up, but rather than make them the Council agreed to "block" them at about £20 a mile; "nothing has come of it" (the OCR breaks off there; the rest of the leader wasn't found).
- ST Sat 3 Dec 1892, p2 a038 (leader): five contested divisions poll on Tuesday; Grierson didn't know he had been nominated for Aithsting, so McCullie has it. The paper backs S. T. Duncan in Sandwick: "no member of the last County Council was more devoted" to his constituents; he came to almost every meeting "often on wild and stormy days" seeking roads for Levenwick and the West Side, and "It certainly not his fault" no roads have been made. Calls his opponent John Tulloch the nominee of a party opposed to the crofters.
- ST Sat 26 Nov 1892, p3 a045 (The Nominations): nineteen seats unopposed, five contested, three with no nomination "either from accident or carelessness".
