# Party labels: Lerwick Town Council, 1914–1924

Researched 2026-09-30 (queue: Labels 2). Same method as `party-labels-ltc-1890-1913.md`: manifest
sweep of the issues around each general, the cited result and nomination reports, and searches
for 1921–24. The 1919–21 narrative is in `early-socialists-1901-1913.md`. BNA link pattern:
`https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0000666%2FYYYYMMDD&page=NNNN&article=NNN`.

## 1914 (election 69): unopposed, no labels

## 1915–1916: wartime appointments by the Council

Elections 70–74 were votes of the Council (Sinclair 6 v Sutherland 2 in 1915, Henderson 6 v
Manson 2 in 1916) or unanimous appointments. No one stood under a label.

## 1919 (election 75)

- ST 25 Oct 1919 p1 a148 (and 1 Nov p1 a194): mass meeting of trade unionists, Rechabite Hall, Fri
  31 Oct, "To hear Addresses by Labour Candidates for ensuing Local Elections", signed James Laing.
- ST 1 Nov 1919 p1 a190: public meeting of ratepayers, Town Hall, Mon 3 Nov, "TO HEAR ADDRESSES BY
  LABOUR CANDIDATES". The notice doesn't name them.
- ST 8 Nov 1919 p4 a087 "A Record Poll": "The majority of the candidates stood in groups". Ramsay,
  H. J. Robertson and C. B. Stout stood together with a circular; Goodlad, Ollason and Harrison
  stood together; J. T. J. Sinclair alone. "Messrs James Laing, J. J. Pottinger, M. L. Manson and
  James Morrison stood together, and addressed a public meeting in the Town Hall on Monday
  evening", chaired by W. Sinclair.
- **Label: Labour** for Laing, M. L. Manson, Pottinger and Morrison, basis `inferred`: the report
  names the four and the Monday Town Hall meeting; the notice for that meeting names it as the
  Labour candidates'. The other two groups had no name.

## 1920 (election 76)

- ST 30 Oct 1920 p1 a164: joint address to the electors "as Labour Candidates", pointing to "the
  work of Labour Representatives on the Town Council"; signed David Murray, William Bruce (the
  first signature is lost in the OCR).
- ST 30 Oct 1920 p1 a177: public meeting, Rechabite Hall, Mon 1 Nov: addresses by the "LABOUR
  CANDIDATES: Mr William Sinclair, Mr David Murray, Mr William Bruce, and Labour Members of the
  Town Council".
- ST 6 Nov 1920 p4 a095: "The three Labour candidates" addressed that meeting; "The other three
  candidates — Messrs Ganson, Ratter and Duffin — issued a circular" (no name).
- SN 4 Nov 1920 p4 a028, post-poll leader (checked by James on the page image, 19 Oct 2026): "The
  result of the Town Council election has been the return of the three candidates put forward by
  the Moderate Party, viz., Mr Alex. Ratter, Mr R. D. Ganson and Mr G. H. Duffin, and one of the
  three candidates run by the Socialists, viz., Mr William [Sinclair]". Viewer:
  https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0003210%2F19201104&page=0004&article=028
- **Label: Labour** for Sinclair, Murray and Bruce (read). **Label: Moderate** for Ratter, Ganson
  and Duffin (read, SN 4 Nov 1920): the ST left their circular unnamed, but the SN names them. This
  makes Nov 1920 the earliest "Moderate" label, not Nov 1921.

## 1921 (election 78)

- ST 22 Oct 1921 p4 a089: "rumour has it that the Social-Democrats intend putting forward one or
  two new candidates, while the 'Ratepayers' also have prospective candidates in view".
- ST 5 Nov 1921 p4 a083: "both 'parties' — the Moderates and Social Democrats — issued circulars
  on Monday morning". Neither slate is listed, and no p1 address names a label.
- First labelled `inferred` on James's answer of 2026-09-30 (q08): Bruce, Murray, A. S. Manson and
  W. Pottinger as Social Democrats, the other six as Moderates.
- SN Thu 27 Oct 1921 p4 a062, "Town Council Election. Ten candidates for six seats. Four Labour
  representatives forward." (checked by James on the page image, 19 Oct 2026): all the retiring
  councillors except W. Sinclair renominated (J. T. J. Sinclair, Ollason, Goodlad, J. Smith,
  Ramsay), with the new man John Manson, Burgh Road; "The remaining four are Labour candidates,
  viz., Messrs David Murray, Wm. Bruce, Alex. S. Manson, and Wm. Pottinger".
  https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0003210%2F19211027&page=0004&article=062
- SN Thu 3 Nov 1921 p4 a053: the fight lay "between the Moderates and the Socialists".
- **Label: Labour** for Bruce, Murray, A. S. Manson and W. Pottinger (read, SN 27 Oct; James,
  19 Oct 2026: Labour, not Social Democrat). **Label: Moderate** for J. T. J. Sinclair, Ollason,
  Goodlad, J. Smith, Ramsay and John Manson (read, SN 3 Nov). The slates are stated, not inferred.

## 1921 by-election (election 77)

J. M. Goodlad v Bruce, 5 votes each, a Council vote decided by the Provost: not a label contest.

## 1922–1924 (elections 79, 80, 82): unopposed, no labels

Nomination and result reports (ST 14 Oct and 4 Nov 1922, 13 Oct and 3 Nov 1923, 25 Oct and 1 Nov
1924) and searches for "labour" found no label. The 1924 by-election (Reid Tait) was unopposed.

**Revisited 2026-10-20.** The Shetland News issues of 12 Oct–16 Nov 1922, 11 Oct–15 Nov 1923 and
9 Oct–13 Nov 1924 (a manifest sweep, with the OCR of every town council or election article
searched for labour, socialist, moderate and ratepayer) give no label either. ST 4 Nov 1922 p5
a283 ("No Poll"): three would-be challengers withdrew, blaming the "lackadaisical" committee behind them.
Contemporary evidence that the sitting members kept their colours:
- SN Thu 11 Dec 1924 p4 a050, special meeting of Tue 9 Dec 1924: "For the first time a Labour
  Provost now occupies the chair" (Laing), and the new Junior Bailie, M. L. Manson, "is also a
  Labour member".
  https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0003210%2F19241211&page=0004&article=050

So the 1922–24 re-elections carry each member's last contested label, basis `inferred` (11 rows):
Laing, M. L. Manson and Morrison Labour, Duffin Moderate (1922); A. S. Manson and Murray Labour,
Ratter and Ganson Moderate (1923); Bruce Labour, J. T. J. Sinclair and Ollason Moderate (1924).
Reid Tait, new in 1924, stays unlabelled. The council reads 6–6 from Nov 1921 to Nov 1924, then
6 Labour, 5 Moderate and 1 unlabelled.
