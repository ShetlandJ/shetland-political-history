# Balfour Spence: where did "Town Councillor 1823" come from?

Started 2026-10-12, finished the same day. Question (James): trace the chain of custody of the
claim that Balfour Spence was elected to Lerwick Town Council in Sept 1823 (ledger row
`balfour-spence` 1823-09-04, `confirmed=0`; background in `answers-2026-10-12.md`).

## Result

The claim starts with the wiki and nothing earlier has been found. James created the wiki page
"Lerwick Town Council Election September 1823" on **17 May 2016**, with Balfour Spence in the
list from the first revision. The page says the information came from the council minutes. His
person page (created 29 May 2016) had "1820 to 1826" from the start. No later edit added it.

The minute book doesn't record the 1823 result: the election minute opens on p27, the burgess
roll fills p28, and the top of p29 is ruled but blank. The wiki list is exactly the ten members
seen at the 1823–24 meetings (p29–33), with Balfour Spence added second, after William Spence.
So the 2016 entry looks like an inference: eleven seats, ten names seen, and Balfour was the
sitting Spence. Nothing in the BNA calls him a councillor or a bailie.

## Correction to the earlier note

`answers-2026-10-12.md` and the ledger source said he was absent from every meeting after
1 Oct 1821. **That's wrong.** He attended to the end of the 1820 council:
- **mb-p25**, 12 Aug 1822: "Councillors, Angus, Jas. Ogilvy, B. Spence, Jas. Mouat, Jas.
  Pottinger, T. B. Nicolson".
- **mb-p27**, 2 Sep 1823 (two days before the election): "Councillors Spence, Angus, Jas Mouat,
  Pottinger and Jas Ogilvy". No William Spence sat on the 1820 council (p22), so this Spence
  is Balfour (inferred).

Taken together with the 1823 burgess roll (p28), he was still active in council affairs at the
election. This makes him a plausible holder of the unaccounted-for eleventh seat, but it doesn't
prove it: he's absent from all five meetings of 1823–24 and from the 1826 sederunt. The ledger
source is corrected; the row stays `confirmed=0` (James's option c).

## BNA, all titles, "balfour spence" (43 hits; 21 with Lerwick): all read

- **1808-10-14** (0000401 p4 a028): a Shetland society's members, with Gifford and Edmondston.
- **1817-07** (London Gazette and reprints): appointed Vice Consul for Hanover in Shetland.
- **1821-08-25, 09-01** (0002370 p8 a045; 0002131 p4 a004): sequestrated, "merchant in Lerwick".
- **1843-07-29** (0000860 p2 a014): "the Vice Consul of the Shetland Islands".
- **1871-04-14/18/20**: death of his son Charles Ogilvie Spence, "second son of the late
  J. Balfour Spence". "J. Balfour Spence" finds only these notices; "John Balfour Spence" finds
  nothing. The "J." is likely a slip.
- **1887-12-03** (Shetland News, 0003210 p5 a056): a reprint of old Lerwick documents, including
  an 1826 weights-and-measures jury (Lerwick, 12 Oct 1826). He's "Mr Balfour Spence, Merchant

## Relatives' pages (2026-10-12)

- **Charles Spence** (ZCC Yell South 1958–64, d. 22 Sep 1996): his site bio's "great grandfather
  ... an early Lerwick Town Councillor" is the wiki's own claim, not a source. His obituary (ST
  4 Oct 1996 p15 a145, "Death leaves void in Yell") is where the rest of his bio comes from, and
  it doesn't mention Balfour Spence or his ancestry. Charles's page said "great grandfather"; he was the
  great-great-grandson (James), fixed in `fix_parse_errors.py` #31.
  https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0000666%2F19961004&page=0015&article=145
- **William Spence** (1782–1849): a distant cousin who sat with the 1823 council. He died before
  the Shetland Times began, and nothing about him would be expected to name Balfour as a
  councillor. Not searched.
