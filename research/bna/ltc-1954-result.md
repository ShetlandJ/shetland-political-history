# LTC May 1954 result

Researched 2026-09-27 in the British Newspaper Archive (Shetland Times, a Friday paper by then).
Every link opens the BNA viewer on the article; click **Articles** in the toolbar for the OCR text.

Question: the wiki has the four May 1954 winners as unopposed, but there were eight candidates
(see `ltc-election-dates-1949-1964.md`). What was the result?
Answer: a poll of 1913 out of an effective electorate of 3918. The four winners were the wiki's
four; the losers were Senior Bailie John N. Inkster, Robert Strachan, J. B. A. Sutherland and
James R. Gair. Fixed by `fix_newspapers.py` #8. On the way, the 1951 "James Inkster" turned out
to be John N. Inkster. No term_issues cleared (53 before and after): the winners' ranking gives
no short seats, since four were elected to four vacancies.

## Evidence

### The result
- **ST Fri 7 May 1954, p4 (art. 048)**: "Senior Bailie loses his seat". The OCR is unreadable, so
  this was read from a zoomed page image. THE RESULT box:

  | Candidate | Votes |
  |---|---|
  | \* A. Morrison (Soc.) | 1031 |
  | Miss G. Halcrow (Ind) | 1013 |
  | \* R. B. Blance (Soc.) | 1010 |
  | \* R. Ollason (Ind) | 929 |
  | \* J. Inkster (Ind) | 813 |
  | R. Strachan (Soc.) | 719 |
  | J. B. A. Sutherland (Ind) | 704 |
  | J. Gair (Soc.) | 692 |

  (\* = retiring member.) Text: "There are 3950 on the roll, but of these 32 are not eligible to
  vote until the autumn", an effective 3918, and the percentage poll "was 48.85". Hourly figures end
  "8, 1913, including 16 postal votes". (1913/3918 is 48.83%; the DB stores 48.8.) Halcrow is "the
  first woman candidate to be successful at the Lerwick poll for nineteen years". The "rejection of
  Mr John N. Inkster, senior Bailie in the old Council". Strachan and Gair "were also unsuccessful
  last year". Sutherland made "a first attempt as an Independent candidate".
  https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0000666%2F19540507&page=0004&article=048
- Consistent with **ST Fri 14 May 1954, p7 (art. 118)**: Blance "third (only 21 votes behind the
  top-of-the poll candidate)" (1031 − 1010 = 21).
- Edits: votes on the four winners (was "Unopposed"); four losing candidacies added (Inkster →
  `john-inkster-ii`, Strachan → `robert-strachan`, Sutherland and Gair unlinked with the Bayanne
  links their later candidacies already use: I86195 as in 1962, I52866 as in 1953); electorate
  3918 with the roll detail, turnout 1913. Party labels as the wiki gives the same people (Soc. =
  Labour). Ledger: Morrison, Blance and Ollason's 1954–57 rows confirmed (they were three of the
  four retiring in 1957, per `ltc-1955-1965.md`).

### 1951: "James Inkster" was John N. Inkster
- **ST Fri 13 Apr 1951, p4 (art. 047)**: "Election—five for four seats". The candidates were the
  four retiring members and Andrew M. Mouat. Nominations: "JOHN N. INKSTER, Cairnfield";
  "Mr Inkster has been junior Bailie in the Council".
  https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0000666%2F19510413&page=0004&article=047
- **ST Fri 13 Apr 1951, p6 (art. 068)**: council business, "Bailie Inkster and Councillor Conochie".
  https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0000666%2F19510413&page=0006&article=068
- Edits: the May 1951 candidacy "James Inkster" (unlinked) becomes "John N. Inkster" →
  `john-inkster-ii` (John Nicolson Inkster, who sat from 1947). Ledger row relinked, and the four
  1951–54 rows confirmed: the four retiring in 1954 are these four (three re-elected, Inkster
  defeated).
