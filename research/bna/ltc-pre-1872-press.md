# Lerwick Town Council before 1872: the press

The pre-Shetland Times sweep (queue, "Pre-1872 town council sweep"). The minute book
(`ltc-1818-1871-minute-book.md`) is the record of the council; this file keeps what the papers
add to it: things the book leaves out, gaps in the book, and quarrels between councillors.
Reports that only repeat a minute aren't listed.

## 1. Orkney & Zetland Chronicle (`0006124`), swept whole 2026-10-01

Printed in Edinburgh ("Printed by John Moir, 21 West Register Street", 20 Sep 1826 p18), not
Kirkwall as the queue had it; the BNA lists it under Edinburgh. All 13 issues in the BNA (31 Jan 1825 to 20 Sep 1826; 568 articles) read from the OCR endpoint
and filtered for Lerwick, the magistrates and council, and the councillors' names. The run sits
almost exactly on the minute book's one long silence: no meeting is minuted between 13 Dec 1824
(p33) and 6 Sep 1826.

**What the Chronicle doesn't have.** No Lerwick council meeting, election, bye-law or
office-bearer appears in any issue. Its Shetland news in 1825 is the landholders' campaign for a
Zetland valuation roll and the county vote (already used for Gilbert Duncan and Alexander Irvine,
`biographies-ltc-1818-1850.md`), the 1826 Westminster election, fisheries, antiquarian pieces
and the Zetland Club in Edinburgh. So the gap in the book isn't filled from here: no evidence
either way that the council met in 1825.

### Greig v Edmondston (1821–1826): two councillors at law

James Greig (writer, Procurator Fiscal of Zetland; councillor 1818–20 and 1823–35, Bailie 1832–35) sued Dr
Arthur Edmondston (Bailie 1818–1823) for damages for defamation. They had sat together on the
council from its first election in 1818 until 1820.

**Caledonian Mercury, Sat 10 Jun 1826, p4 (art. 012)**
https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0000045%2F18260610&page=0004&article=012
"Jury Court. Action of Damages": "On Wednesday last, the 7th of June". Damages laid at £2,000;
"an unanimous verdict for the pursuer—Damages, £300". Cockburn and Jeffrey for Greig.

**Inverness Courier, Wed 14 Jun 1826, p3 (art. 006)**
https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0000446%2F18260614&page=0003&article=006
"Jury Court, Wednesday, June 7": an action for "alleged libel contained in a letter printed and
published" by the defender, addressed to Sir William Rae. Unanimous verdict for the pursuer.

The trial was on **Wednesday 7 June 1826**: the Chronicle's "Wednesday the 8th" (OCR "Bth") is
the Chronicle's slip or the OCR's; 7 June 1826 was a Wednesday.

**Orkney & Zetland Chronicle, 31 Dec 1825, p5 (art. 014)**
https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0006124%2F18251231&page=0005&article=014
"The trial by Jury in the process of damages Mr James Greig, writer, Lerwick, against Dr Arthur
Edmonstone" fixed to come on once witnesses can be brought from the country.

**Orkney & Zetland Chronicle, 20 Jun 1826, pp1–7 (arts. 001, 004, 005, 008, 009, 012, 013)**
https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0006124%2F18260620&page=0001&article=001
"Notes of Trial" before the Jury Court in Edinburgh (dated "Wednesday the 8th June", see above); Jeffrey and Cockburn
for Greig, J. A. Murray and Skene for Edmondston; the Lord Chief Commissioner on the bench.
- The issues (p2, art. 004): in 1823 Edmondston printed and published a letter to Sir William Rae,
  Lord Advocate, following an official letter of August 1821 on "the conduct of Mr James Greig".
  It accused Greig of acting for the Crown and for his brother-in-law Francis Heddell on opposite
  sides of the same cause and taking fees from both.
- The cause (p3, art. 005): a man named Morrison had tried to build a pier below high-water mark
  in front of his house at Lerwick. Greig's predecessor as fiscal, Mr Duncan, challenged it; the
  Sheriff found against Morrison about twenty years before; the Board of Customs defended that
  judgment in the Court of Session in the fiscal's name. Jeffrey's case was that Greig had
  refused to take any part and his name was used "as a form, contrary to his will".
- A Sheriff Court action of 1821 between them came first: Edmondston's printed letter complains
  that the Sheriff found him liable in damages to Greig (20 Sep 1826, p7, art. 022, quoting
  p21 of the pamphlet).
- **Verdict** (p7, art. 013): "Verdict given for pursuer, L. 300 damages."

**Orkney & Zetland Chronicle, 20 Sep 1826, pp6–13 (arts. 019–037)**
https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0006124%2F18260920&page=0007&article=022
The report continued ("Evidence for the Pursuer"): the pamphlet, Edmondston's 1821 defences and
the Sheriff-substitute's interlocutor put in; the Sheriff-substitute examined (fiscal before
1806, then Sheriff-substitute, with Greig succeeding him as fiscal). This was the Chronicle's
last issue in the BNA.

Supports: biography additions for `arthur-edmondston` (his intro calls him "notoriously
litigious"; this is the case) and `james-greig` (`fix_newspapers.py` #72). No ledger row
changes. Edmondston left the council at the 1823 election, the year of the pamphlet; the book
doesn't record that result (see the minute-book file), so nothing links the two.

### Ledger consistency found on the way

The #71 death-date fixes (Paterson 8 Apr 1828, Archibald Greig 12 Oct 1852) hadn't reached the
ledger: their terms still ended on the 5th and the 11th. Both end dates moved to the death date,
as every other `died` row has.

## 2. Shetland Journal (`0005738`, Jun 1836 – Nov 1837), swept whole 2026-10-01

Arthur Anderson's paper, printed in London (Richard Taylor, Red Lion Court, then Jacob Unwin). Every issue in the BNA: 11 Jun, 10 Sep and 12 Nov
1836; 2 Jan, 1 Feb, 4 Mar (not the 1st), 1 Apr, 1 May, 1 Jun, 1 Jul, 1 Aug, 1 Sep, 31 Oct and
30 Nov 1837 (every day of 1836–37 probed; every article read). Read it as Anderson's voice: its
leaders and letters take his side (steam to Shetland, against the Lerwick merchants who ran
things), and the news pieces are written from Lerwick by his friends.

**What it doesn't have.** No council meeting, election or office-bearer is reported, though the
minute book has twenty meetings in Jun–Aug 1836 (Heddell's and Hay's claims on the beach below
the street, `transcripts/080`–`088`). The minute book is silent from 22 May 1837 to the
6 Sep 1838 election (p93); the Journal has one council item from that gap (below).

**Shetland Journal, 10 Sep 1836, p3 (art. 018): "The Shetland Mail"**
https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0005738%2F18360910&page=0003&article=018
Resolutions of "a numerous Meeting of the Inhabitants" at Lerwick (day lost in the OCR), signed
by Charles Ogilvy as chairman and, from the garbled heading, as chief magistrate: the Peterhead
sailing packet had been kept seven days at Peterhead loading cargo for its contractors; the mail
contract should be opened "to public competition"; a committee of C. Ogilvy, J. Barclay,
J. Hunter and two others. Anderson's paper prints it "although we do not entirely concur": he
had just told the Post Office commissioners in London to pay a steamer (the Sovereign) to carry
the mail in summer.

**Shetland Journal, 1 Feb 1837, p3 (art. 030): letter, "the Shetland nightmare"**
https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0005738%2F18370201&page=0003&article=030
A correspondent mocks Lerwick's public life: nothing gets done unless it suits "one or two of our
local magnificoes"; then a meeting is got up and "cut and dry resolutions" passed. His example is
the Peterhead packet resolutions of No. 2 (the 10 Sep 1836 meeting above), "said to be passed at
a numerous meeting of the inhabitants of Lerwick". Partisan, and anonymous; colour, not fact.

**Shetland Journal, 1 Aug 1837, p4 (art. 045): "Proclamation of the Queen at Lerwick"**
https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0005738%2F18370801&page=0004&article=045
When the Journal reached Lerwick "on the sixth inst." with the account of Victoria's proclamation
in London, the town held its own the same evening at six: a procession "of our magistrates, town
councillors, and some other local notables", the proclamation read in front of the town-house,
three cheers, and the magistrates drinking the Queen's health inside. A royal salute from Fort
Charlotte was dropped: the bombardier wouldn't fire without orders, and the shock might bring
down a new house built under the walls. The letter is undated; "the sixth inst." is July
(Victoria was proclaimed in London on 21 June), so **Thursday 6 July 1837** (`inferred`). Not
in the minute book (no meeting minuted 22 May 1837 – 6 Sep 1838). Council at the time: the
1835 council (Bailies C. Ogilvy and G. Duncan).

**Shetland Journal, 1 Sep 1837, p4 (art. 031): letter from Lerwick, 23 Aug 1837**
https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0005738%2F18370901&page=0004&article=031
A meeting "attended by all the principal people of the town" on the 8th (Tue 8 Aug 1837)
agreed to petition the Postmaster General for a steam mail packet. The writer complains of "one
important omission": no thanks to the Journal's editor or to Anderson. The quarrel of 1836 the
other way round.

Supports: no ledger row changes. The proclamation is the only council act found in the 1837–38
gap; it could go in the open-questions file if James wants it on the site (e.g. a sentence in
Charles Ogilvy (ii)'s biography).
