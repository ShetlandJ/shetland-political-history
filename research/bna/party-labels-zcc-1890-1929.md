# Party labels: Zetland County Council, 1890–1929

Researched 2026-09-30 (queue: Labels 5). Method: manifest sweep of the Shetland Times for the
nominations issue, the issue before polling day and the result issue of every general (Feb 1890,
then December every three years to 1929), reading the County Council, election and p1 address
articles; the cited nomination and result reports from `zcc-1890-1919.md` and `zcc-1920-1939.md`;
and one search. BNA link pattern:
`https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0000666%2FYYYYMMDD&page=NNNN&article=NNN`.

**Result: County Council elections were fought without labels.** No nomination list, result
report or candidate's address from 1890 to 1929 names a party or slate, apart from one
candidate who called himself independent. Most by-elections were appointments by the Council on
a ratepayers' petition, with no label either.

## 1901 (election 294, Tingwall)

- ST 30 Nov 1901 p1 a009: George Leslie's address to the electors of the Tingwall division: "I
  would remind you that I am an entirely independent Candidate". **Label: Independent** (new).
  William Duncan, who won, had no label.

## 1919 (elections 443, 457)

- The DB (from the wiki) has James J. Pottinger (Lerwick Central) and William Sinclair
  (Whiteness and Weisdale) as **Labour**. Both stood as Labour for the Town Council a month earlier
  (`party-labels-ltc-1914-1924.md`), but the County Council nominations (ST 22 and 29 Nov 1919),
  the result (6 Dec) and a search for "labour county" over 8 Nov–13 Dec 1919 give no label for
  either. Unconfirmed, not contradicted (open question).
- James Laing (Lerwick South, unopposed) and the other Lerwick members had no label.

## 1920–1929

- The Lerwick divisions (Laing, Mouat, J. T. J. Sinclair, White) were unopposed or unlabelled in
  1922, 1925 and 1928; Lerwick North 1925 (Mouat v Captain George Bruce) was unlabelled.
- No labels in the Dec 1925, Dec 1928 or Dec 1929 generals.

## Aside for the LTC (1906)

ST 10 Nov 1906 p4 a059 ("Return thanks" column): "Mr James Laing, who has been returned [as a]
'working man' candidate, now enters public life for the first time". Added to the open question
on Laing's 1906 label.
